### Consulting (Quansight / OpenTeams)

I spent the better part of 2025 doing what everyone was doing, which is building AI agents. I worked
for an AI startup based in the US (there are 15000 of them at this point), who were building
an AI platform for large Enterprises, to accomplish everything an entry level employee can do, to be able
to replace them. I got introduced to [LangGraph](https://github.com/langchain-ai/langgraph) and [LangChain](https://github.com/langchain-ai/langchain), building custom agents / orchestration of
a bunch of agents to create AI workflows. One of the most annoying parts of this was, the space was (and is)
moving so fast that if you're using a version of a package which was barely a week old, you could
experience completely unexpected behaviour, as a lot of it is maintainers trying to catch up with other
maintainer's releases and they catching up with [OpenAI](https://openai.com) and [Anthropic](https://anthropic.com) APIs. One of the most interesting
agents I was working on was browser use agents, to automate daily browser tasks, by letting AI control the browser.

A major part of the remaining year was spent on working on sovereign AI solutions. Ever since the acquisition of
Quansight consulting, the vision at OpenTeams has been to enable enterprises and governments to own their AI through transparent, open-source solutions - ensuring they can deploy systems they fully control and trust. The work ranged from deploying open weights models like Qwen on edge to k8s clusters
on cloud to build user interfaces to make it accessible for the end user.

### AQI Monitor

I was in Delhi during the end of the year, when the AQI was 400 on an average. This is the first time in a while when
I found Delhi's air extremely unbreathable. We have a few air purifiers in the house, but the sensor in all of them
are pretty bad, they start running in low power mode when they "think" the AQI is low and if you run the air purifier
in turbo mode all the time, then it's very inefficient, as the HEPA filter would wear out pretty quickly.

To solve this I was looking for an AQI sensor / monitor that I can program such that, I can start/stop air-purifiers
based on accurate AQI values. I started looking for some aqi monitors on amazon, didn't find a single monitor which
fit the requirements, so decided to build one. I found [Sensirion's SEN55](https://sensirion.com/products/catalog/SEN55) sensor, which can measure PM, RH/T, VOC, NOx at
an accuracy more than good enough and gold standard for my use case. Paired with an [ESP32](https://www.espressif.com/en/products/socs/esp32) and [Home Assistant](https://www.home-assistant.io/), I
can now automate air-purifiers and monitor it from anywhere in the world, as it's useful to know what my parents are
breathing.

Below is a photo of the monitor, at the moment it doesn't have a screen to display live AQI readings, but I plan to
add one soon. The component on the top of the plastic case is an ESP32. The case was 3D printed from a publicly
available source, but it didn't fit the larger esp32 in this case. I plan to make it prettier in future, but for
now it does the job.

### Webhook to Phone Call API

A simple Go service that turns HTTP requests into phone calls using [Twilio](https://www.twilio.com/). I built this for emergency notifications and alerts from my homelab and servers. When something critical happens, I get an actual phone call instead of a notification that I might miss.

Check it out: [aktech/webhook-to-phone-call-api](https://github.com/aktech/webhook-to-phone-call-api)
